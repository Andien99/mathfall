# Mathfall

A co-op classroom roguelike. Students form teams of four, each on their own device, and fight through a branching dungeon to a boss. Every Attack, Defend and skill only fires when the student answers a maths question correctly.

This is milestone 1: the full loop works end to end over real multiplayer (one server, many teams). The art is generated in code; see *What's real and what isn't* at the bottom.

## How a game runs

1. **Teacher** opens `/host`, picks questions (school bank, own sets, generated topics, or a mix), sets options and clicks **Create game**.
2. The lobby shows a big join code, the site address and a QR code. **Students** go to the site, type the code and their name, then **choose a hero**: Knight, Mage, Ranger, Cleric or Samurai, with a **Boy / Girl** switch above the cards that changes the hero's look (not its stats). Teammates see each other's picks. Then they tap **Lock in**: their card gets a green *✓ Locked in* stamp, the other cards dim, and a green bar says *You're the Mage! Waiting for your teacher to press Start*. **Change hero** unlocks it again at any time before the game starts. Teammates see *✓ Locked in* or *still deciding* beside each name, and the teacher's lobby shows the same on every student plus a count ("12 students joined · 9 locked in"). Locking in isn't required: anyone still deciding when the teacher presses Start keeps the hero they picked (or gets a free class). In a solo test game, locking in starts the run.
3. Students are grouped into teams of four as they join (team size is a setting). **Balance teams** spreads them evenly; drag a student to move them; drop on the dashed box to make a new team. Click **Start game**.
4. Each team gets its own **tower map**: a tall parchment scroll climbed from the bottom floor to the boss at the top. Six paths wind up through seven columns, merging and splitting, so most rooms offer two or three ways up. Everyone votes for the next room by tapping it; the room most of the team picks wins. Rooms: Monsters, Elite, Treasure, Campfire, Merchant, Unknown (ambush, shrine or cache) and the Boss. Floor 1 is always monsters, the middle floor is always treasure and the last floor before the boss is always a campfire. Elites and campfires never appear in the bottom third. Run length sets the height: Quick 6 floors, Standard 9, Long 13 (plus the boss). Enemies scale with how far up the tower you are, not the floor number, so all three lengths end equally hard.
5. **Battles** run in rounds, and everyone acts in the same round:
   - Pick **Attack**, **Defend** or one of your skills (2 at the start, up to 3). A question opens. A correct answer fires the action at once.
   - **Energy** (0–10) powers skills. Attack and Defend each charge +3; each Focus answer charges +1. Common and rare skills cost 2–6 energy, legendary skills 8 or more (the Knight's Champion's Combo costs 9). Heroes start each fight with 2, so the opening round is always a basic action.
   - A **wrong answer is a miss**: the hero swings and whiffs, and their turn is spent. The answer card drops to the bottom of the screen so the miss plays in view above it, with the correct answer and a *Show the working* button. After a correct answer the card closes at once and the hero's action plays only then, so students on phones see their own attack. Switching action before answering keeps the same question, so students can't skip hard ones.
   - **Free follow-up:** a correct answer for Attack or Defend makes the student's *next* Attack or Defend free (no question). The one after that needs a question again. Skills always need a question.
   - **Quick-time events:** elites and bosses sometimes unleash a big move (the Sentinel's Earthshatter, Gravemaw's Tail Storm and Shatterstorm). Everyone gets a times-tables question, multiplication or division up to 12 × 12 (factors 2 to 12, e.g. `7 × 8` or `56 ÷ 8`; `qteQuestion` in `shared/content.js`), with 10 seconds to answer; a right answer dodges the hit completely. Some raid moves chain three in a row. Before the prompt opens, the camera closes in on the enemy, which freezes on the frame its attack starts from (wind-up, roar, or the Minotaur's head-down charge stance) with a slow red pulse; the prompt appears about 1.3 s later, so the 10 seconds start when students can see it. The enemy stays frozen through every prompt, then strikes first in the enemy turn, straight out of that pose, as the camera eases back (`qteFocus` / `qteRelease` in `scene.js`, `QTE_LEAD` in `server/session.js`).
   - When every hero has acted, or the round timer runs out, the enemies act. Each enemy shows what it will do next (for example "8 → Mia" or "Storm next!"), so the team can react: guard a target, freeze or kill a dangerous enemy, heal.
   - An unanswered question carries into the next round, so no work is lost.
   - Students who finish early can answer **Focus** questions: each correct one makes their next attack or skill 30% stronger (up to three).
   - A knocked-out hero answers a question to **get back up**. Everyone who fell revives at the end of a fight.
   - Tap an enemy to target it. Tap a teammate (or their card) to guard or heal them.
6. **After every won fight** each student picks an upgrade: **Attack** (+15% damage), **Defence** (+15% block, 6% less damage taken) or **Health** (+6 max HP). Upgrades last for the run, and a team that beats the tower keeps its heroes (upgrades, skills, gold) for the next, harder climb.
7. **"?" rooms** are one of ten events, each with an illustration and two or three choices, several of them risk-or-safe: Wyvern's Nest, Strangling Vines, Shrine of Numbers, The Grinning Dealer, Forgotten Library, The Suspicious Chest, The Fallen Adventurer, Moonlit Fountain, Abandoned Forge and Ambush! (a team decision: fight together, or everyone sneaks past by answering correctly). Choices marked ✎ ask a question; the outcome depends on the answer.
8. **Campfire**: the party sits around the fire; each student picks Rest (heal 35%), Train (upgrade a skill) or Meditate (start the next fight with full energy and Focus). Bubbles show what everyone chose.
9. **The raid.** When the game timer ("Time until the raid") runs out, or when the teacher presses *Start raid now*, every team is pulled into one fight against **the Inferno Dragon, Heart of the Tower**. Heroes below 80% health are healed to 80%. Each team sees its own party in front, with every other team's heroes fighting in the background; all damage goes into one shared health bar (about 130 HP per student, and it hits 20% harder than a normal boss). The dragon uses quick-time attacks, sometimes three in a row. A team that falls regroups at half health. The class wins if the dragon falls before the raid timer (default 8 minutes) ends; results then show damage by team and by student.
10. **Skills, items and loot.** Every skill belongs to one class: each class has 16: its 2 starters, plus 6 more common, 5 rare and 3 legendary skills found in chests and shops (only ever its own class's). Heroes start with 2 skills and can carry 3; a 4th must replace one. **Items** are passives any class can carry, with no limit (15 of them: Lucky Coin, Whetstone, Energy Crystal, Sturdy Boots, Chalk Pouch, Bandage Roll, Thorned Mail, Focus Lens, Healer's Satchel, Vampire Fang, Spare Potion, Troll's Belt, Phoenix Plume, Sage's Crown, Infinity Pencil). Every chest and shop slot rolls its rarity **50% common (grey border), 30% rare (purple), 20% legendary (orange)**. **Treasure**: each student opens a chest of one rarity holding some gold (35 / 55 / 85 by rarity, +15 from an elite), which is always theirs, plus a skill and an item, of which they keep one. **After each act's boss** every student gets a legendary chest (the boss's hoard, +30 gold): its skill is always one legendary skill of their class (one they don't have yet, while any are left), then the next act starts. The **Merchant** sells each student 2 skills of their class and 2 items (about 40 / 70 / 120 gold by rarity) and mends 40% health for 30. The **Campfire** heals 35% or upgrades a skill (+35% power). `node test/skills.test.js` checks every skill and item against its description.
11. **Acts.** Each map ends in a boss. Beating it completes the act: the team keeps its heroes and climbs a new map (Act 2, Act 3, …) with a different boss at the top and tougher enemies. The bosses rotate: Gravemaw the Crystal Wyrm, then **The Iron Colossus** (overheats at half health and hits harder; telegraphs a quick-time *Meltdown*), then **The Hollow King** (raises Bone Thralls, drains life with *Soul Drain*, and calls a three-strike quick-time *Death Knell*); after that the cycle repeats, stronger each time.
    - **Boss health follows the team.** An act boss's health is linear in the heroes playing (3 heroes get 75% of a 4-hero boss; it used to be 81%, so smaller teams took longer). On top of that, every won fight records how fast the team actually deals damage per hero per minute, with recent fights counting most, so upgrades, skills, items, accuracy and answering speed all count. The boss is then given enough health to last about 2½ minutes of fighting on Quick, 3¼ on Standard and 4 on Long (plus 15 s per later act), kept between 50% and 115% of the size-based health. A team needs 2 fights and 45 s of measured fighting first; before that the size-based health is used. If a hero drops out (or comes back) for 15 s during a boss fight, the boss's health is rescaled to the heroes still playing, keeping the same fraction of the bar. Summoned shards and thralls are unchanged. Each boss's sizing is in the session report (`boss` events); the rules are in `bossPaceFactor` / `recordPace` in `server/combat.js` and `test/boss.test.js` checks them.
12. The **first boss**, Gravemaw the Crystal Wyrm, changes at half health: it summons two Crystal Shards and charges *Shatterstorm*, which hits the whole party harder for every shard still standing. Break the crystals first.
8. Beating the boss starts a harder run (Ascension 1, 2 …). A team that falls starts a new run. Teams keep going until the teacher ends the game or the time limit runs out.
9. **Results**: the teacher gets team standings, every student's accuracy, best streak, average time and weakest skill, class accuracy by skill (weakest first) and the hardest questions, plus **Download results (CSV)**. Each student sees a podium and their own report with the questions they got wrong and the worked answers.

While playing, the teacher's **live view** opens with **the tower**: a drawn stone tower with battlements, the boss chamber at the top (showing the bosses teams are facing), lit windows on floors where a team is, and every floor from 1 to the boss, with each team's banner on the floor it has reached, its heroes, and what it is doing right now. Click any team to show its battle on the projector. During the raid it becomes the raid board: the Inferno Dragon's health and damage by team. Below that, the live view shows every team's floor, current room, enemies, hero health and accuracy. **Watch** opens a team's battle on the projector. **Pause all** freezes every team ("Eyes on your teacher"). **Hurry along** ends the current round or room for a team that's stuck.

## Game options

| Option | Choices |
|---|---|
| Run length | Quick (~10 min), Standard (~20 min), Long (~30 min) |
| Round timer | Off, 30, 45, 60 or 90 seconds |
| Team size | 2 to 5 |
| Adaptive support | On/off: after two wrong in a row, a student gets supported questions until they get two right |
| Hints | On/off: a hint button on every question |
| Pause after a wrong answer | none to 5 seconds |
| Game length | No limit, or 15 to 60 minutes |
| Sound on student devices | On/off (students can also mute) |
| Pawcal's tips | On/off: the guide cat (below) |
| Late joining | Allowed or locked once started |

## Questions

### The school bank

Every `.json` file in the `banks/` folder appears under **School bank** for every teacher. This is the "agreed upon" bank: add or change a file, redeploy, and everyone sees it. `banks/year-7-algebra-core-skills.json` is included: 43 questions plus 29 supported questions on like terms, collecting like terms, coefficients and constants, substitution and equivalent expressions, each with a worked solution. It leaves out brackets (expanding and grouping) and keeps squares simple (no harder than $a^2 + 4$; no square roots).

`banks/year-10-probability-mix.json` (*Year 10 Probability (8A–8F)*) has 88 probability questions from Cambridge Essential Maths 10/10A, 8A–8F: basics, experimental probability, set notation, Venn diagrams, two-way tables, the addition rule (17), conditional probability (20), two-step tables and tree diagrams. 49 have a diagram. Every question is a short step or answered by inspection, or a rule to recall or complete. No supported list. The diagrams are SVGs in `banks/img/y10-probability/`, drawn by `node tools/build_prob_images.js`; the set is built by `node tools/build_y10_prob_bank.js` (it checks every picture exists). The file name is kept from when it was mixed with algebra, so saved selections still work.

### My sets

Teachers' own sets are saved in their browser. **New set** opens the editor:

- Write maths between dollar signs: `$3x + 5x$`, `$\frac{1}{2}$`, `$x^{2}$`. The preview shows exactly what students see.
- *Pick from options* (correct answer plus up to three wrong ones; options are shuffled per student) or *Type the answer* (with a **Test it** box to check what will be marked right).
- Optional skill (for the results page), hint and worked solution.
- **Picture (optional):** a diagram, table, graph or spinner shown under the question. **Add a picture** picks a file (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or SVG), or paste a screenshot with Ctrl + V anywhere in the question box, or paste an `https://` link. Big pictures are shrunk to 1000 px wide (about 50–250 KB each) and saved with the set in your browser, so a set with many pictures can fill the browser's storage; **Export** keeps a copy. Add a short description for screen readers.
- Every set has a **main list** and a **supported list**. Drag questions between them, or use ⇄ to copy.
- **Paste a list**: one question per line, parts separated by `|` or a tab: `question | correct | wrong… | skill`. With only a correct answer it becomes a typed question.
- **Import a file**: Mathfall or **Flurry Fight** `.json` exports, `.csv` or a text list.
- **Export** saves a `.json` copy. To add a set to the school bank, put that file in `banks/`.
- **Copy and edit** on a school bank set makes your own editable copy.

### Generated topics

Integers, Solving equations, Index laws, Percentages, Fractions, and Like terms and substitution. Each has Foundation, Core and Extension levels, fresh numbers every time, wrong options built from common mistakes, and a worked solution for every question. Students on supported questions get the level below. Mix them with sets to stretch a small bank.

### Supported questions

A student uses the supported list when the teacher clicks the small dot beside their name (lobby or live view), or automatically for a few questions after two wrong in a row (if adaptive support is on). The dot has no label, so a projected screen doesn't reveal who is supported, and students never see it. Dots are remembered by name in the teacher's browser.

### How typed answers are marked

Accepted: spacing and capitals, term order (`6 + 8k` = `8k + 6`), letter order (`ba` = `ab`), equal numbers (`1/2` = `0.5`, `1 1/2` = `1.5`), a leading `x =`, a `$` sign, a unit (`26 cm`), and `−` or `×` typed with the keypad.

Marked wrong on purpose: unsimplified answers (`3k + 5k` for `8k`), brackets left in when the answer is expanded (or missing when it's factorised), `×` or `÷` when the answer has none, and an unevaluated sum (`40/8 + 8`) when the answer is one number.

### File format

```json
{
  "title": "Year 7 Algebra: core skills",
  "questions": [
    { "type": "mc", "skill": "Collecting like terms", "q": "Simplify $3x + 5x$.",
      "options": ["$8x$", "$15x$", "$8x^{2}$"], "correct": 0,
      "hint": "Add the numbers in front.", "solution": "$3 + 5 = 8$, so $8x$." },
    { "type": "text", "skill": "Substitution", "q": "If $m = 5$, evaluate $4m + 3$.", "answers": ["23"] }
  ],
  "modified": [ ...supported questions in the same shape... ]
}
```

`hint`, `solution` and `image` are optional. `image` is a path inside `banks/` (`"img/y10-probability/venn-5-2-6-3.svg"`), an `https://` link, or a `data:image/…` picture (what the editor saves); add `imageAlt` to describe it. Flurry Fight exports use the same shape.

**Question pictures.** Students see the picture under the question (and again, smaller, with the worked answer after a miss); tapping it opens it full screen. Uploaded pictures are sent to the server once when the game is created and served to students as a short `/qimg/…` link (kept in memory, sandboxed so an SVG can't run script); school bank pictures are served from `/banks/img/`. The teacher's results page and each student's report show the picture beside the question. `test/images.test.js` checks the rules and that every bank picture exists.

## Run it on your own computer

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 18 or newer.

```
npm install
npm start
```

Open http://localhost:3000/host. Students on the same network join at `http://YOUR-COMPUTER-IP:3000`. School networks often block this, which is why hosting it online is usually easier.

To test multiplayer on one computer, open the student page in several **separate windows or browser profiles** (each tab keeps its own player, so ordinary tabs work too).

## Put it online (free, on Render)

1. Create a GitHub repository and upload this folder (everything except `node_modules`).
2. Sign in to [render.com](https://render.com) with GitHub, click **New → Blueprint**, choose the repository and click **Apply**. `render.yaml` sets up a free Node web service in Singapore (lowest lag from Australia).
3. Render gives you an address like `https://mathfall.onrender.com`. Students go there; you go to `/host`.

**Free-plan catch:** free services sleep after a while without visitors and take up to a minute to wake. Open the host page a few minutes before class. One free instance comfortably handles a class of 24–30; everything is turn-based and the server sends small updates.

## Good to know

- Games live in the server's memory. Restarting or redeploying ends any game in progress.
- Up to 48 players per game, in up to 10 named teams.
- A student who refreshes or drops out rejoins automatically as the same hero, with their open question restored. Rejoining with the same name also reclaims a disconnected hero. The teacher's page also resumes after a refresh.
- A disconnected student never holds up their team: rounds and votes only wait for connected players.
- The server marks every answer and resolves every action. Correct answers are never sent to the browser, each question can only be answered once, and duplicate or stale submissions are ignored.
- Sound effects are 8-bit WAVs in `public/sfx/` (85 sounds, about 820 KB), made with **sfxr** (the classic retro game-sound tool, via its JavaScript port jsfxr) by `node tools/build_sfx.js`. Each sound is one sfxr patch or a few layered ones; the jingles (correct answer, chest, victory, defeat, battle start) are short tunes on NES-style square and triangle-like voices. `tools/build_sfx.js` also sets each sound's loudness (`LEVEL`) and writes `public/sfx/manifest.json`. The legendary skills (Hero's Resolve, Phantom Volley, Deadeye, Divine Shield, Dragon's Wrath, Time Stop, Resurrection) have their own sound for each beat of their animation. Hits, slashes and arrows play at a slightly random pitch so repeats don't sound identical. Builds are repeatable (sfxr's noise is seeded from each sound's name). `/dev/sounds.html` is a sound board for hearing every effect. The files download when the page opens and play after the first tap (iPads need one tap before sound can play); until then, or if they fail to load, a small built-in synth plays instead. To change a sound, edit its patch in `tools/build_sfx.js` and re-run it (or `node tools/build_sfx.js hit coin` for just those).
- The teacher's live view only redraws the parts whose data changed (at most once a frame), holds redraws for up to a second while the teacher is scrolling, and stops drawing the Watch battle once it's closed. The page background and header no longer repaint on scroll.
- The battle scene drops to a lighter mode by itself if a device struggles. Try it on a typical school Chromebook before a lesson.

## Pawcal, the guide cat

Pawcal is a pixel cat in a scholar's cape who pops in at the side of the screen with one short tip at a time (`public/js/guide.js`). He sits beside the party card on laptops, just above the action list on phones, and at the top left on the tower map, so he never covers the rooms being voted on. He never shows while a question is open. Each tip is shown once per device; students can tap **Got it**, turn tips off with **No more tips**, or press the **?** button in the top bar at any time to hear the most useful tip for that moment again (which also turns tips back on). Teachers can switch him off for the class under *Pawcal's tips*, and **Reset tips** in the test-game panel makes him say everything again.

- **Battle:** what to press for Attack and Defend (keys `1` `2` on keyboards, tap on tablets) and that a right answer makes the move happen; the free follow-up after a correct Attack or Defend; what the enemy intent tags mean and how to target; Focus (`F`) while waiting; getting back up; and a warning on the first boss.
- **Skills:** the first time a student can afford a skill, Pawcal points at it (the button pulses) and says what it does and its key. Damage skills are suggested first. If energy is full, he says extra energy is wasted. If a student uses Attack or Defend twice in a row with a skill affordable, he nudges them (at most twice a fight, 40 s apart, and he stops once they have used skills a few times). Affordable skills also shimmer in the action list all the time.
- **Rooms:** the tower map vote, skills vs items at the first chest and the shop, the campfire choices and the upgrade after a win, plus a cheer when a new skill is learned.

The sprite sheet (`public/art/guide.png`, 10 frames of 64×64: idle loop, talking, pointing, cheering) is built from PixelLab frames in `tools/art-src/guide/` by `python3 tools/build_guide.py`: `cat_base.png` from Create Image (Pro) with the mage as the style reference, the idle and talking loops from `animate_image`, the point and cheer poses from `edit_image`.

## Skills and items on the loot screens

Chest, merchant and boss-hoard cards show **what a card is** by its colour and **how rare it is** by its border. Every card has a coloured ribbon and body for its type: **SKILL** is blue ("You use it in battle": a square rune tile with the energy cost, the hero class and pips for your 3 skill slots), **ITEM** is green ("Always on by itself": a round relic frame, "any hero", "No energy, no slot, no limit"), and **GOLD** is yellow ("Spend it at the shop"). The 3 px border is the rarity: grey common, purple rare, orange legendary (gold takes its chest's rarity). **Legendary** cards have an animated border: two bright streaks of light run round it while the card's glow pulses (still for anyone with reduced motion turned on).

A chest gives everyone its **gold** (35 / 55 / 85 by rarity, +15 from an elite, +30 from a boss) plus a choice of its **skill OR its item**. The gold sits on its own yellow card under *Yours to keep*, apart from the choice: students tap **Collect** to bank it (coins fly to the gold counter and the card turns to *✓ Collected*), and any gold not collected is paid when the room closes, so nobody can lose it. Beside it, under *Choose one*, the skill and item cards have a round **OR** between them, with *No thanks, keep what I have* underneath. If a student picks first, the gold card stays up with "Don't forget your gold!" and they are marked ready once they collect it. The line above reads "Collect your gold, then choose one: a skill OR an item". On phones the gold card sits above the skill OR item pair. The merchant has no OR: you can buy as many as you can afford. The loadout strip under the cards says *Your skills: used in battle* and *Your items: always on*.

## Replacing the art

The built-in sprites are generated by a pixel rig in `public/js/sprites.js` (heroes, enemies, boss, chests, icons) and the dungeon by `public/js/dungeon.js`. To use hand-made art, put sprite sheets in `public/art/` with a `manifest.json`:

```json
{
  "heroes": {
    "knight": { "sheet": "knight.png", "frame": [84, 76],
      "poses": { "idle0": [0, 0], "idle1": [1, 0], "idle2": [2, 0], "idle3": [3, 0],
                 "windup": [0, 1], "swing1": [1, 1], "swing2": [2, 1], "cast0": [3, 1], "cast1": [4, 1],
                 "defend": [0, 2], "hurt": [1, 2], "down": [2, 2], "victory": [3, 2] } }
  },
  "enemies": {
    "slime": { "sheet": "slime.png", "frame": [64, 52],
      "poses": { "idle0": [0, 0], "idle1": [1, 0], "idle2": [2, 0], "idle3": [3, 0], "windup": [4, 0], "attack": [5, 0], "hurt": [6, 0] } }
  }
}
```

Hero frames are 84×76 with the feet at (46, 72), facing left. Enemy frame sizes are in `ENEMY_DEF` in `sprites.js`, facing right. Anything not listed keeps the generated art. Add `"portrait": [x, y, w, h]` to a hero to crop its HUD portrait from the idle frame. Add `"variants": 3, "variantRows": 1` to put colour variants on extra rows.

**The Samurai** (a red panda in a crimson haori) is the fifth class: *Precision*, 40 HP, Iai Cut (8) and Parry (8 block). Its signature is **Poised**: Still Mind, Kiai, Perfect Parry and Inner Calm make the samurai Poised, and the next basic attack or damaging skill deals double damage (blocks and buffs don't spend it; `spendsPoise` in `server/combat.js`). The rest of the pool rewards timing: Quickdraw and Moonlit Iai hit harder against an enemy at full health, Final Cut and Moonlit Iai against one below half. Its sprite is PixelLab concept A1 (`tools/art-src/samurai_base.png`, mirrored to face left), built by `python3 tools/build_ranger.py samurai:m` into 14 placeholder poses (lean, recoil, crouch, kneel) in crimson, indigo and jade (only the coat is recoloured, since the fur shares its reds). For now the Boy and Girl picks are the same red panda (`samurai:f` is an alias in the manifest), and it has no hand-animated moves yet, so it uses the generic dash-and-swing. **Samurai idle:** a low iaido crouch with a paw on the hilt of the sheathed katana, ready to draw (after Andy's ink reference), breathing with a swaying tail (a PixelLab idle loop, `tools/art-src/samurai_idle.png`); the strike frames use the blade-out stance, so he draws as he hits. **Quickdraw** (after Andy's slash FX and dash references): he springs into an airborne dash with his paw on the sheathed hilt (a PixelLab keyframe), lands in his iaido crouch by the enemy, a red aura gathers round him, the draw-cut lands with a red crescent slash (`public/art/fx/quickdraw_slash.png`, a PixelLab image revealed and faded in code) and red shards, he holds the follow-through, re-sheathes with a click and goes back (`_iaiAnim` in `scene.js`, `anims.iai` built from `tools/art-src/quickdraw/`). **Samurai victory** (after Andy's victory concept sheet): out of the crouch he draws and raises the blade high, slashes the air (PixelLab's own smear arc; the game throws a gust of cherry blossom forward on `drawAt`), flicks and sheathes it (`sheathAt`: from the click, blossoms drift down around him, tumbling and settling, for as long as the win lasts; `_drawPetals` in `scene.js`), turns his back, takes the straw kasa off his back and puts it on with the brim pulled low over his eyes, and holds the pose with the sheathed sword at his side, breathing. Keyframes are PixelLab `edit_image` (the turned hold pose is shrunk to his height with the head kept full size by `python3 tools/build_samurai_victory.py`), in-betweens `animate_image`; the sources are in `tools/art-src/victory/`, and `python3 tools/build_attack.py samurai:m/victory` builds the sheet (the coat is recoloured by crimson vs orange, so the fur never changes colour). **Legendary skills darken the background** for the whole move: the background dims at 0.84 (the old charge dimming was 0.42 over everything), while the fighters and the effects stay at full brightness; the room's lights, light shafts and top shading dim with it, so a hero leaping high isn't washed out (`darkFor` / `DARK_A` in `scene.js`).

These hand-timed moves are in `EXTRA` in `build_attack.py` (`charge` / `chargeTo` for the growing light, `swing` for the trail, `ring` for the volley's shrinking ring, `aura`, `dashAt` and `upswing` for Power Strike). The volley's raise was pinned to a near-vertical keyframe made with `edit_image`, because PixelLab's free animation would only aim the bow to about 45°.

PixelLab paints its own magic into many frames; `tools/assemble_moves.py` keeps only the character (the largest connected shape plus anything touching the idle sprite's box), so detached swirls and orbs are dropped, and strips the fire it drew above the mage's staff. The frame order and holds per move are in its `PLAN`; timings and hit frames in `SIMPLE` in `build_attack.py`. Move sheets load in the background after the page opens; the join form can no longer submit (and reload the page) before the game has loaded.

**Animation Lab and preview pack.** `/dev/lab.html` (live at https://mathfall.onrender.com/dev/lab.html) replays any hero's attack, defend, starting skill or any skill from their class pool against any enemy (the real event the server would send, from `public/dev/skill-events.json`; rebuild it with `node tools/skill_events.js` after changing a skill), with every effect, at 1×, ½× or ¼× speed, boy or girl, any colour; it never touches a real game. It also takes settings from the address bar (`?cls=mage&gender=f&move=fireball&enemy=slime&speed=0.5&loop=0&bare=1`). `/dev/icons.html` shows every skill icon by class and every item icon, with their rarity borders. `/dev/bossbadges.html` shows each boss's round tower-map badge (head and shoulders cut from its idle frame; `BADGE_FACE` in `sprites.js` pins the face for the current art). `python3 tools/make_previews.py <folder>` (with the game running locally) builds an offline pack: sprite-only GIFs of every hero animation, MP4 battle clips of every move with effects (recorded from the lab), enemy GIFs, event art, the raw sprite sheets and an `index.html` gallery.

**Hit sparks.** Every damaging hit throws long streak sparks that fly well out and arc down (`hitSparks`); the knight's blows also throw tumbling steel-blue cubes.

**The Minotaur (Act 1 boss).** Andy's PixelLab minotaur replaced Gravemaw as the first act's boss. `python3 tools/build_minotaur.py` turns his four 128×128 frames (`tools/art-src/minotaur_src.png`: stand, roar, lunge, fists raised; already facing the heroes) into `public/art/minotaur.png`, lined up on the feet, with a breathing idle, wind-up, lunge attack, roar and hurt pose, plus two PixelLab move animations made from his frames (`tools/art-src/minotaur_moves/`): `slam0-6` and `run0-7`. **Ground Slam** (`_minoSlam` in `scene.js`): fists up, he slams the floor (big shake, dust, a ring of small spikes), jagged glowing cracks race to every hero and PixelLab earth spikes (`public/art/fx/earth_spike_*.png`) burst up along each crack in growing pairs, the biggest right under the hero, throwing them up. **Stampede** (`_minoCharge`): head down and pawing the floor, he gallops through the whole party with an after-image trail and dust (each hero is hit as he passes, or leaps over him if they won the quick-time), runs off the right edge and comes back in from the left. Both can be replayed in the Animation Lab under *Enemy moves* (with *Full party (4)*). He fights Gravemaw's two-phase fight under his own names (`MINOTAUR_MOVES` in `server/combat.js`): Horn Gore, Stampede (quick-time), Ground Slam and Iron Hide; below half health an Earthshaker Roar raises two Crystal Shards that power a Shatterstorm unless the team breaks them first, then a Crushing Blow. Gravemaw is still in the game files but no longer appears.

**Portrait phones and tablets.** When a device is held upright, the question docks to the bottom of the screen with no dark overlay, so the battle stays visible above it. Tested with a party of four on a 390×844 phone and an 820×1180 iPad: on the iPad the battle keeps its full size and slides up while a question is open (about 250 px), which puts every fighter above the question; on a phone the question card fills about 60% of the screen, so sliding still hid two heroes and most of the boss. Phones therefore keep the fighters in the band under the party cards all the time, with the heroes in two staggered rows. Add `?pv=pan` or `?pv=top` to the address to force either behaviour.

**Load (24-player stress test).** `STRESS=1 node test/bots.test.js 24` plays 24 bots (6 teams of 4) through full runs and the raid, and reports reply times, bytes sent to each device and server CPU/memory; add `REAL=1` for real game speed with bots that think 4-12 s per question, like a class. Results on a 2.8 GHz server core: at real speed the server averaged 0.6% of a core (peak 4%), used 75 MB of memory, replied to answers in 1 ms typically (53 ms worst) and sent each student about 4 KB/s (the whole class about 94 KB/s, the teacher's screen 21 KB/s). Render's free plan gives 0.1 of a core and 512 MB, so that is about 25x headroom on CPU at the peak. The fast, instant-answer version (8x game speed) peaked at 15% of a core: a worst case no class reaches. The tower map is now left out of updates when it hasn't changed (the client keeps its copy), which halved the bytes per update.

**Test game (solo).** On the host's setup screen, **Test game (solo)** (beside *Create game*) creates a game with the chosen questions and settings and opens it in a new tab, already joined as "Teacher"; picking a hero starts the run straight away, with no lobby or second device, and the host tab shows the live view as usual. **Invincible heroes** (ticked by default) means enemy hits show their numbers but take no health (`h.invincible` in `hitHero`). A pink **TEST** button in the game opens shortcuts to any room: monsters, harder monsters, elite, boss, boss to half health (phase 2), treasure, merchant, campfire, unknown event, the victory and act-complete screens, full energy, low health (to try heals) and the raid boss. These shortcuts only work in test games (or with `MATHFALL_TEST=1` for the automated tests).

**Battle result screen.** After a win (and after a boss) the rewards slide in on the left in a teal glass panel, modelled on a classic JRPG results screen: **Battle Result** (or **Act Complete**), then Gold obtained (counts up), Health recovered and Items obtained (the elite's treasure chest, the boss's legendary chest, or a blank line), with the upgrade choice and the ready line underneath (`resultScreen` in `play.js`). The battle stays visible on the right, where the heroes celebrate. On phones and portrait screens the heroes celebrate first: the camera zooms in on the party for about 2.6 s, then the rewards rise from the bottom (tap to skip) while the camera eases back so the party stays framed above them; the party cards step aside while it's up.

**Victory animations.** Andy's own victory GIFs for the boy knight, cleric, mage and ranger (`tools/art-src/victory/<sheet>.gif`) are lined up with each hero's battle sprite by `python3 tools/build_victory.py` (the boy cleric is shrunk to 93% like his battle sprite), then built into move sheets by `build_attack.py` (`anims.victory`). Each plays once, then holds its final pose with a slow 1-pixel breathing loop (`loop` in the manifest; `_animAt` in `scene.js`). The celebration waits for the last blow to finish. The girls' versions are the same animations re-dressed by PixelLab `transfer_outfit` (each boy frame repainted as the matching girl, using her battle idle as the reference), so they move exactly like Andy's, frame for frame; the girl mage's magic is tinted to her orange fire. Their frames are in `tools/art-src/victory/<sheet>/`. Heroes are never faded during the victory, even if a player has dropped out.

**Enemy art (PixelLab).** Every enemy is a PixelLab "Create Image (Pro)" sprite drawn with the heroes as the style reference: Goblin Raider, Goblin Hexer, Mire Slime, Skeleton Warrior, Bone Thrall, Crystal Shard, the Ironbound Sentinel, and the bosses (the Minotaur, the Iron Colossus, the Hollow King and the Inferno Dragon). The chosen candidates are in `tools/art-src/enemies/`; `python3 tools/build_enemies.py` mirrors any that face left, puts the feet on a fixed anchor and makes the poses from the one drawing (breathing idle, wind-up, lunge, hurt, charge, roar), writing `public/art/<kind>.png` with its own frame size and anchor in the manifest. The hand-drawn enemies in `sprites.js` are now only a fallback.

**Enemies shudder when hit.** Every time an enemy takes damage it shakes side to side seven times over about 0.56 s, 4–9 px each way (`hitShake`), harder for bigger hits; poison and burn ticks give a small shake, and other teams' raid hits a faint one. All ten event illustrations in `public/art/events/` were generated with PixelLab (192×192, shown with a soft vignette). The raid boss, the Inferno Dragon (internal id `voidmaw`), is Andy's red dragon, made in PixelLab: `python3 tools/build_dragon.py` turns `tools/art-src/dragon_base.png` (256×256, mirrored to face the heroes) into `public/art/voidmaw.png`, scaled to 148px and snapped back to its own palette, with lean, lunge, roar and hurt poses and fire breath added at the jaws.

**The raid arena: a lava cavern.** The dragon's sprite stands on its own rock, so the raid isn't fought in a dungeon hall: it has its own theme, `inferno` (set in `play.js` and `host.js` whenever `run.raidMode` is on). The back wall, the floor and the lava are PixelLab "Create Image (Pro)" paintings in `tools/art-src/inferno/` (basalt cliffs and columns with two lava falls, 384×176; a cracked basalt floor with glowing cracks, 384×128, plus a deeper 384×256 version for phones and tall screens; a molten lava surface with floating crust, 160×80). `python3 tools/build_inferno.py` copies them to `public/art/bg/` (the lava is mirrored into a 320 px strip so it tiles as it scrolls). `paintInferno` in `dungeon.js` lays them out for any screen size (mirrored copies cover wide screens) and paints a lava pool with a dark rock shore round the dragon's feet (`bossSpot` in `scene.js`, so it always matches where the dragon is placed). In battle (`drawInfernoBack` / `drawLavaLip` in `scene.js`) the pool's lava flows slowly sideways and bubbles, the lava falls run, embers drift up off the pool and the floor cracks, and heat glows up from below instead of the dungeon's light shafts. After the dragon is drawn, the front of the pool is drawn again over the foot of its rock, column by column with a lapping edge, so the rock rises out of the lava; the lava line glows and flickers against the rock, small wavelets run away from it, wisps of smoke rise off it, and the bottom of the rock is lit orange from below. Because of that, the dragon no longer has a floor shadow, doesn't slide forward for Void Rend (its attack pose leans in), shakes at most 4 px when hit, and melee heroes stop on the shore (58 px in front of it, `reachDX`). Its all-party attacks now throw fireballs from its jaws instead of crystal shards. Until the art has loaded the raid shows the lair. `/dev/scene.html?raid=1` previews the arena with 20 other-team heroes in the background (`?raid=8` for 8).

**The knight is hand-made.** `public/art/knight.png` is built from Andy's knight (`tools/art-src/knight_base.png`, 64×55, facing left) by `python3 tools/build_knight.py` (needs Pillow). The script cuts out the sword and re-angles it around the grip (RotSprite-style, so the blade stays crisp), leans and shifts the body for each pose, makes the kneeling knocked-out pose, and recolours the cape for the second and third knight in a team (blue, crimson, emerald). To change the knight, replace the base image and re-run the script. `/dev/sprites.html` shows every frame; `/dev/scene.html` previews the battle scene.

## Session report (for feedback after a lesson)

**Session report** on the live view, and **Download session report** on the results page, save a `.json` file describing how the game ran. Send it to Claude with the results CSV and a few lines of notes ("lag around minute 6", "two iPads dropped out") for feedback.

- **Server, every 10 s:** players connected, CPU (% of one core for the whole server; Render's free plan gives about 10%), memory, event-loop lag (the clearest sign the server is struggling), the slowest 200 ms game tick, data sent and received, how many devices are on WebSocket vs long-polling (polling usually means the school network is interfering), and what each team is doing.
- **Each student device, every 10 s:** frame rate, ping, how long each Attack, answer and quick-time reply took to come back, whether the battle dropped to lower detail, time spent on another tab, browser errors, and the device (browser, screen size, cores).
- **Events:** joins, rejoins, disconnects (with the reason), kicks, pause, raid start and end, server errors.
- **Summary:** peaks and averages of all of the above, the five slowest devices and the five highest pings.

The report lives in the server's memory with the game, so download it before closing the results page; a restart or redeploy loses it. As a backup the server writes a one-line summary to its log every minute and a final summary when the game ends (`[mf-telemetry]` lines in Render's logs). The report is behind the game's host token, so only the teacher's page can download it. Recording costs almost nothing: one small message per device every 10 s. Built in `server/telemetry.js` and `public/js/telemetry.js`; `test/bots.test.js` checks the report.

## Tests

```
npm test
```

Runs 65 answer-marking cases, then starts a test server and has bot students play full runs over real sockets (8 bots, two teams), including a disconnect and reconnect and a duplicate-answer check. `node test/bots.test.js 24` runs a full class of 24 in six teams.

## What's real and what isn't

**Built and tested:** real multiplayer for many teams on one server; simultaneous rounds with a round timer; server-side marking; worked solutions; adaptive supported questions; hints; Focus; getting back up; 5 heroes with 3 colour variants each; 80 class skills across 3 rarities (up to 3 per hero) and 15 passive items, powered by energy (charged by Attack, Defend and Focus) with upgrades; 5 enemy types with telegraphed intents (including a healer and an elite with a two-turn wind-up); a two-phase boss; a Slay-the-Spire-style tower map (6, 9 or 13 floors, 7 columns, 6 merging paths) climbed bottom to top with team voting; Andy's hand-made knight sprite with 13 animation frames in 3 colours; treasure (skill, item or gold), campfire, merchant (2 skills + 2 items), shrine; ascension runs; teacher editor, import/export, school bank, generators, lobby, live view, spectating, pause, results and CSV; reconnects.

**Prototype quality:**
- **Art.** The sprites and dungeon are generated from code, not hand-drawn. They're consistent and animated (idle, attack, cast, defend, hurt, knocked out, victory), with smooth lighting, fog and particles over the pixel art, but they won't match a commercial game's hand-painted sprites. The manifest above is the route to commissioned or bought sprite sheets.
- **Balance.** Bots won comfortably; real students will be slower and less accurate. Expect to tune enemy health and damage (`shared/content.js`, `server/combat.js`) after the first lessons.
- **Phones.** Laptops, Chromebooks and iPads (both orientations) are the target. Phones work in landscape but are cramped.

**Not built yet:** a live-editable shared bank (needs a database; for now the school bank is the `banks/` folder); saved results history (download the CSV); music.
